# Teach

Build demonstrable capability for a stated practical outcome. Measure progress
by what the learner can explain or do, not by the amount of material covered.

## Establish the learning boundary

Confirm the learner's goal, current experience, constraints, and immediate
mission. Use a small diagnostic question or task to distinguish demonstrated
knowledge from assumed fluency. Gather trustworthy primary sources and separate
source facts from teaching simplifications.

## Teach through use

1. Introduce one tightly scoped concept in the context of the mission.
2. Give a concrete example and one boundary or failure case.
3. Ask the learner to retrieve the idea, predict an outcome, or perform a small
   task without copying the explanation.
4. Give immediate, specific feedback tied to the evidence in their answer.
5. Adjust the next step to the demonstrated gap or strength.

Keep lessons short and use the learner's own domain where possible. Cite claims
that depend on external facts. Record only durable references, decisions, or
practice results that will be useful later; do not create a diary by default.
Propose that record and write it once the learner agrees. Do not modify an
existing learning record without asking.

Finish by checking the learner can apply the concept to the original mission and
by stating the next useful practice step. Ask before changing the mission or
expanding the agreed learning record.
